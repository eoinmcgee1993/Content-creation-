# Recording script

About an hour of material once trimmed. Record it over a few short sessions
with the setup in the README kept exactly the same each time. One file per part
or per prompt is fine; the checker adds them up.

How to talk, all the way through:

- **Like you're on the phone to a practice manager you get on with:** friendly,
  clear, your normal pace. ElevenLabs copies the style it hears, so this is the
  voice the clone will have.
- **Don't tidy up the accent or turn it up.** It will copy either.
- **If you slip,** pause, then say the whole sentence again. Cut the slip later.
- **Leave a second of silence** at the start of every take. The checker
  measures the room's noise in it.

## Part A: the accent passage (about 3 minutes)

This passage uses every English vowel sound, plus the contrasts Irish English
keeps and other accents lose: *for/four*, *horse/hoarse*, *which/witch*. It also
has plenty of *th*, *l* and *t*-between-vowels. Read it twice, once at your
normal pace and once a little brisker.

> Last Thursday morning I got the bus into town from Douglas. The driver was in
> great form, though he said it was the worst weather he'd seen in years. I got
> off on the Grand Parade and walked up Patrick's Street, past the English
> Market, with the rain blowing sideways off the river.
>
> My brother Tom was waiting outside the bookshop with two coffees and a bag of
> hot chips. "You're late again, boy," says he. "I thought you'd be here by
> four." I told him the bus was stuck behind a horse box on the Kinsale road the
> whole way, but he didn't believe a word of it.
>
> We sat down by the water and watched the swans go under the bridge, over the
> noise of the traffic. There were three of them, and a couple of young lads in a
> boat, fishing for nothing in particular. Tom said the price of a pint in this
> city is a disgrace. I said it's the same in Dublin, only worse.
>
> Then we argued about hurling, the way we always do. He thinks Cork will win the
> All-Ireland next year. I think he's half mad, but I'd love him to be right. Our
> father was the same: calm about everything except the match, which left him
> hoarse every Sunday.
>
> By the time the bells rang out from Shandon it was nearly dark, so we went
> looking for food. Which was a mistake, because the only place still open was
> full of tourists asking where the Blarney Stone was, and which bus would take
> them to Cobh. Sure look, we had a good laugh about it anyway. I was home by
> half nine, wet through, and happy enough.

## Part B: the caller's lines (about 5 minutes)

These are the kinds of things the clone will say on calls, following the call
brief in `clearmark_voice.py`. Say each one as if the person has just picked up.

**Opening**

- Hi, is this Siobhán? I'll keep this really quick.
- Hi there, is that Dr Murphy? Sorry to catch you in the middle of the day.
- Morning! Have you got thirty seconds? I promise that's all it is.
- Hi, I'm an AI assistant calling for Clearmark. Is this the practice manager?

**Why you're calling**

- I was looking at your Google Ads account, and a fair chunk of the spend is
  going on clicks that never turn into bookings.
- That pattern usually means some of the budget is converting at a loss.
- I noticed on your website that a few of your pages are sitting just off the
  first page of Google.
- What I'd like to do is send you a free Account Health Check. It scores two
  hundred and fifty points, and it shows you exactly where the spend is leaking.
- Or there's a free Search Console health check. It maps the keywords you're
  nearly ranking for, and the searches where people see you but don't click.
- It takes about a minute to read, and there's no catch.

**Details**

- What's the best email to send the sample report to?
- So that's S, I, O, B, H, A, N, at gmail dot com? Perfect.
- And just to check, was that one-two-three, or one-two-two?
- Could I take a number in case we get cut off? Zero eight seven, four five six,
  seven eight nine zero.
- Would Tuesday at half ten suit you, or is Thursday afternoon better?
- That's about twelve percent of last month's spend, so roughly four hundred and
  eighty euro.
- Is there someone else who looks after the marketing? Who'd be the best person
  to talk to?

**Closing**

- No problem at all, I'll let you get back to it.
- Totally understand. Would it be all right if I emailed it over anyway, and you
  can look at it whenever suits?
- Sorry, I didn't catch that. Would you mind saying it again?
- Ah, you're grand. Thanks for your time, have a good one.
- That's brilliant, thanks a million. You'll have it in the next few minutes.
- Hi, this is a message for the practice manager. I'm calling from Clearmark
  about your Google Ads account. I spotted something worth a look, so I'll send
  it over by email. Thanks, bye now.

## Part C: talk unscripted (2–3 minutes each, most of the hour)

This is where the Cork rhythm and melody come from, because they only happen in
natural speech. Pick a prompt, hit record, and talk to the mic as if you've rung
someone you get on with. Don't plan it. Ums and restarts are fine. Afterwards,
cut only the false starts and any pause over about two seconds.

1. Give someone directions from Kent Station to the English Market, the long
   way round.
2. Describe your local: the place, the regulars, what you'd order.
3. Tell the story of the best (or worst) match you've been to.
4. Talk through a normal Monday, from the alarm to dinner.
5. Describe a holiday that went wrong.
6. Explain your job to someone who has never used the internet.
7. Explain to a dentist, in plain words, why paying for clicks that never book
   an appointment is money wasted.
8. Walk a practice manager through what Google Search Console shows them, as if
   you're on the phone.
9. Talk about a mistake you made at work and how you sorted it out.
10. Recommend three places to eat in Cork and say why.
11. Describe the house you grew up in.
12. Tell a story about a teacher you remember.
13. Explain hurling to an American.
14. Leave a long voicemail for a friend about plans for the weekend.
15. Ring a business (pretend) to complain politely about a bill.
16. Describe the drive from Cork to Kinsale, or wherever you'd go on a Sunday.
17. Talk about something you changed your mind about.
18. Explain to a new business owner why their website isn't bringing in calls.
19. Describe the best night out you've had in Cork.
20. Say how you'd spend a free Saturday in the city.

For more than an hour (ElevenLabs' best results need 2–3), go round again with
prompts of your own. Keep the same setup and the same phone-call delivery.
