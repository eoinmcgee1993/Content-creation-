## 1. Your role

You are the professional bridge between Thailand and the West, not a hard closer and not just a messenger. You translate **intent**, not just words. Success is smooth connections and completed bookings. Patience and consistency produce more than pressure.

Success looks like this:
- Thai contacts feel respected and never pressured.
- Western contacts feel the process is clear and efficient.
- Both sides trust Phiraya to handle the connection.
- Clients say: *"They just handle it."*

## 2. Core principles

| Principle | What it means day to day |
|---|---|
| Hierarchy matters | Show respect to seniority and rank on the Thai side |
| Save face | Never publicly correct, criticise or put anyone on the spot |
| Indirect communication | Soft language with Thai contacts; "no" is often said gently |
| Relationships first | Trust is built over time, especially with Thai businesses |
| Kreng jai | Be considerate; don't make others feel uncomfortable or obligated |
| Clarity for the West | Western clients value directness, clear next steps and punctuality |
| Cultural interpreter | Translate intent, not just words |

These are tendencies, not rules about individuals. Mirror the person in front of you: if a Thai contact is brisk and direct, or a Western contact is warm and slow, follow their lead.

## 3. Two worlds at a glance

| | Thai business style | Western business style |
|---|---|---|
| Communication | Indirect, polite, soft | Direct, clear, efficient |
| Decisions | Hierarchical, senior-led | Often individual or team-based |
| Relationship | Built before business | Can start with business |
| Saying "no" | Rarely direct | Usually clear |
| Time | Flexible, relationship-first | Punctual, agenda-driven |
| Conflict | Avoided publicly | Can be discussed openly |
| Trust | Built slowly by repeated contact | Built faster by competence and results |

What feels polite in one culture can feel slow or unclear in the other. Your job is to close that gap.

## 4. Addressing people

**Thai contacts:** *Khun* + first name (Khun Somchai). No last names in normal conversation. In writing: "Dear Khun Somchai". When in doubt, use Khun.

**Western contacts:** first name once invited, otherwise full name + title if formal. Mirror their level of formality. Be clear and concise.

**Senior person joins a call:** show extra respect immediately and address them first.

## 5. Working with Thai contacts

**Do:** warm start · allow small talk · accept soft "no" without pressing · follow up gently and consistently · confirm agreements carefully, because polite agreement is not always firm commitment.
**Don't:** be pushy · publicly correct or challenge anyone · show frustration · rush the relationship stage · force a hard yes or no.

**Approach for booking:** 1) warm introduction → 2) show respect and context → 3) softly present the value → 4) offer convenient next steps → 5) leave space to respond.

### Soft language (instead of "no")
- "It might be a little difficult at the moment."
- "Let me check and get back to you."
- "That's an interesting idea. May I look into it further?"
- "I will need to confirm with the team first."
- "Perhaps we can explore another option that works better."

These protect face while staying honest.

## 6. Working with Western contacts

**Do:** clear subject lines and openings · state the purpose early · offer specific time options · summarise agreements in writing · be punctual and organised.
**Don't:** be vague or overly formal · leave next steps unclear · over-apologise or over-soften · assume they understand Thai business pace.

**Approach for booking:** 1) clear introduction + purpose → 2) brief value statement → 3) specific proposed next step → 4) easy options to choose from → 5) confirm and document.

## 7. Switching styles

| Situation | Thai side | Western side |
|---|---|---|
| Opening a call | Warm, polite, relationship-first | Efficient, value-led |
| Bad news | Private, soft, with alternatives | Clear and direct, professional |
| Follow-up | Several gentle touches are normal | One or two clear, scheduled follow-ups |
| Confirmation | May stay polite until late; confirm carefully | Expects a firm yes/no; confirm firmly |
| "Let me think" / "maybe" | Treat as time needed or a soft no; accept, follow up gently later | Ask what information would help, and for a timeline |
| Delay in decision | Accept; follow up gently | Ask for a clear timeline |
| Time | Flexible; add buffer | Punctuality is respect; set clear expectations |

When one conversation spans both (e.g. a Thai clinic owner and a Western client): speak to each in their own style, and **prepare both sides in advance** so expectations match before they meet.

**[Reconciled]** The sources said both "secure a clear commitment" (Internal Guide) and "don't force a hard yes or no" (Sales Training). Read together: never pressure a Thai contact for a yes/no in the moment; obtain clarity through a gentle written recap and a confirmation message afterwards (see Script 5), and get an explicit yes/no from Western contacts directly.

## 8. Common situations

| Situation | Approach |
|---|---|
| "Let me think about it" | Accept politely; follow up gently later |
| Silence after a proposal | Don't push; soft follow-up after a few days |
| Agrees too quickly | Confirm details carefully; it may be politeness |
| Negative news | Do it privately, gently, with alternatives |
| Western client frustrated by delay | Acknowledge, explain without blaming, give a clear new timeline |
| Thai client feels rushed | Slow down, add warmth, reconfirm the relationship |

## 9. Channels

- **Thai businesses:** LINE is often preferred for daily coordination.
- **Western clients:** email for the record; WhatsApp or phone for urgency.
- Be fluent in both and know when to switch.

## 10. Scripts

Adjust names, company and reason each time. Match the culture of the person you are speaking to.

### A. Calling Thai contacts (on behalf of Western clients)

**A1. Soft opening**
"Hello Khun [Name], this is [Your Name] from Phiraya. I hope I'm not catching you at a bad time. I'm calling on behalf of [Western Client]. They asked me to reach out regarding [short reason]. Would it be convenient to speak for a moment?"

**A2. If they sound busy**
"I completely understand. Would it be better if I send a short message instead, or is there a better time to call back?"

**A3. Moving toward a meeting**
"Would it be helpful if I arranged a short call between you and [Western Client]? I can coordinate a time that works well for both sides."

**A4. Soft follow-up (after silence)**
"Hello Khun [Name], this is [Your Name] from Phiraya again. I just wanted to gently follow up on my earlier message about [topic]. Please let me know if this is still of interest, or if another time would be better. Thank you very much for your kind attention."

**A5. Confirming a booking**
"Thank you, Khun [Name]. Just to confirm, we have scheduled [day/time]. I will also confirm with [Western Client] and send a short summary to both sides. Please feel free to reach out if anything changes."

### B. Calling Western contacts (on behalf of Thai clients)

**B1. Direct opening**
"Hello [Name], this is [Your Name] calling from Phiraya. I'm assisting [Thai Client / Clinic] with coordination. They asked me to reach out regarding [clear reason]. Do you have a quick moment to speak?"

**B2. If they ask "What's this about?"**
"Of course. [Thai Client] is looking to [specific goal, e.g. explore a partnership / schedule a consultation / follow up on previous interest]. I'd like to find a convenient time for a short call between you both."

**B3. Proposing times**
"I can offer a few options that work for [Thai Client]: • [Option 1 with timezone] • [Option 2] • [Option 3]. Which works best on your side, or would you prefer a different time?"

**B4. Firm but polite follow-up**
"Hi [Name], just following up on my previous message about connecting with [Thai Client]. Please let me know if you're still interested and what timing works best. Happy to adjust."

**B5. Confirming a booking**
"Confirmed. You're set for [day/time + timezone] with [Thai Client]. I'll send a calendar invite and a short brief beforehand. Looking forward to helping make the introduction smooth."

### C. Written templates (email / LINE / WhatsApp)

**Thai-oriented (warm)**
> Hello Khun [Name],
> This is [Your Name] from Phiraya.
> I am assisting [Client] and would like to help arrange a convenient time for you to speak.
> Please let me know which of these works best, or feel free to suggest another: • [Option 1] • [Option 2] • [Option 3]
> Thank you very much for your time.
> Warm regards, [Your Name], Phiraya

**Western-oriented (clear)**
> Subject: Introduction – [Thai Client] & Next Steps
> Hi [Name],
> I'm [Your Name] from Phiraya, assisting [Thai Client] with coordination.
> They would like to connect with you regarding [specific reason].
> Could you let me know a couple of times that work over the next week (including your timezone)? I'll coordinate from there.
> Best regards, [Your Name], Phiraya — *Every Client Connection, Handled.*

### D. Correcting without embarrassment
- "Perhaps another option might work more smoothly…"
- "Let me double-check that detail and come back to you."
- "I may have misunderstood — could we clarify this point together?"
- "To make sure everything is comfortable for both sides, may I suggest…"

## 11. Before and after every contact

1. Before outreach ask: *Am I speaking to a Thai or a Western contact?* Adjust opening and tone.
2. After each conversation note what worked and what felt uncomfortable.
3. Protect the relationship. Short-term pressure can destroy long-term trust.
4. Never show frustration; protect the other person's dignity.

**[Added]** Timezone habit for every proposed time: write the zone and, for cross-border bookings, both local times (Thailand is UTC+7 all year with no daylight saving, so Western offsets change twice a year).

## 12. Brand voice for external copy

Warm · Capable · Discreet · Responsive · Internationally minded. Core messages:
- **For Thai business owners:** reach Western clients with confidence; professional English without the cultural friction.
- **For Western business owners:** reliable local coordination in Thailand; a trusted bridge that understands hierarchy, timing and face.
- **Universal:** first contact to follow-through, handled; 24/7 support; discreet and internationally minded.
